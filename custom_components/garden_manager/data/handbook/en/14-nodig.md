# No-dig and mulching

With **no-dig** you leave the soil structure intact and feed the soil from the top, as in a forest. It works on every soil and saves a lot of work.

## Making a bed

1. Mow or flatten weeds or grass. Don't dig them out.
2. Lay **cardboard** (no tape or print), overlapping, across the whole bed. Wet it.
3. Spread **10-15 cm of compost** on top.
4. Plant or sow straight into the compost. The cardboard rots in a few months; worms pull the compost in.

## Maintenance

- Every autumn or early spring add **2-5 cm compost** on top.
- Keep weeds small by hoeing just below the surface or pulling them.
- Cut spent crops at soil level: the roots stay and feed soil life.

## Mulching

A 5 cm mulch:

- halves evaporation (less watering);
- suppresses weeds;
- keeps soil cooler in summer and warmer in winter;
- feeds soil life.

| Material | Good for |
|---|---|
| Grass clippings (dried, thin layers) | Tomato, courgette, cabbage |
| Straw | Strawberry, squash, potato |
| Compost | Everything |
| Leaves | Winter cover, paths |
| Wood chip | Paths, perennials (not in vegetable beds) |

Mulch only after the soil has warmed in spring, otherwise it stays cold too long. Slugs love mulch: be careful around young lettuce.
