# Ecological gardening with home-and-kitchen remedies

A healthy garden is a living garden: lots of soil life, many different plants and room for useful animals. Pests never disappear completely - nor should they, because without aphids there are no ladybirds. Hortulus follows this order: **prevent › observe › natural enemies › mechanical › mild remedies**. Chemical control is never in the task list.

## Prevention beats cure

- **Healthy soil:** compost, mulch, no digging, green manures (see chapter Soil).
- **Rotation and good neighbours:** Hortulus warns in the planner.
- **Right place and spacing:** airy crops dry faster, less fungus.
- **Water in the morning at the roots**, never in the evening over the leaves.
- **Resistant varieties** (scab, mildew, blight).
- **Mesh and fleece** as a physical barrier: the most effective eco remedy there is.

## Recipes

| Remedy | Recipe | Against | Mind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft soap | 20 g soft soap per litre (lukewarm) water | aphids, whitefly, spider mite | directly on the pests, in the evening; not in heat |
| Nettle extract (cold) | 1 kg nettles, 10 l water, steep 24 h | aphids | spray undiluted |
| Rhubarb leaf tea | boil 500 g leaves 30 min in 3 l water | aphids, leek moth | toxic - not on edible parts close to harvest |
| Garlic tea | 1 bulb chopped, 1 l boiling water, 24 h | fungi, aphids | dilute 1:5 |
| Horsetail tea | boil 100 g dried horsetail 30 min in 1 l | scab, mildew, rust (preventive) | 1:5, every 2 weeks |
| Milk spray | 1 part milk : 9 parts water | powdery mildew | weekly, in sunshine |
| Baking soda | 5 g bicarbonate + 5 ml oil + a drop of soap per litre | powdery mildew | test on a few leaves |
| Beer trap | sunk jar with beer, rim 2 cm above the soil | slugs | empty daily |
| Board or cabbage leaf | put down at night, collect in the morning | slugs | |
| Wood ash / grit / eggshell | ring around the plant | slugs | only works while dry |
| Coffee grounds | around plants | slugs (a little), ants | slightly acidifies the soil |
| Yellow sticky card | hang between plants | whitefly, thrips, flies | catches beneficials too - use sparingly |
| Corrugated cardboard band | round the trunk from June | codling moth | remove and burn in autumn |
| Grease band | at 1 m height, October - March | winter moth | |
| Fleece / insect mesh | straight after sowing or planting | cabbage root fly, carrot fly, onion fly, flea beetle, cabbage white | seal the edges |
| Water | a strong jet | aphids, spider mite | mites hate moisture |

## Common pests and what works

- **Slugs:** collect at dusk, beer traps, boards, grit, let hedgehogs and ground beetles do the work; raise plants in pots and plant out sturdy ones.
- **Aphids:** water jet, soft soap, beneficials; too much nitrogen makes it worse.
- **Cabbage white and root fly:** insect mesh, brassica collars (cardboard round the stem), celery and tomato as neighbours.
- **Carrot fly:** mesh or a 60 cm fleece fence, onions as neighbours, thin in the evening.
- **Flea beetle:** keep moist, fleece.
- **Whitefly (greenhouse):** yellow cards, Encarsia, French marigolds, soft soap.
- **Spider mite (greenhouse):** raise humidity (damp the floor), predatory mite Phytoseiulus.
- **Late blight:** a roof, water the soil, remove the lowest leaves, resistant varieties, keep potatoes and tomatoes apart.
- **Mildew:** milk spray, airy spacing, regular watering (drought stress).

## Better not

- No salt, chlorine or bleach in the garden: it destroys soil life.
- No metaldehyde slug pellets: toxic to hedgehogs, dogs and cats (ferric phosphate is a last resort).
- "Natural" sprays kill beneficial insects too: spray locally, in the evening, and only when needed.
