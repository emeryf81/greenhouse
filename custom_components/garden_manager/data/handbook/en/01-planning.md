# Planning a season

A good kitchen garden starts at the kitchen table, not in the soil. An hour of planning in winter saves dozens of hours in summer.

## 1. Start with what you eat

List the vegetables and herbs your household really eats, and how much. A classic mistake is too many courgettes and too little lettuce. Guide values per person for one season:

| Crop | Plants per person | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Tomato | 2-3 | Far more reliable under glass |
| Courgette | 0.5-1 | One plant feeds a household of two |
| Lettuce | 10-15 | In 3-4 sowings |
| Carrot | 60-100 | Also for storage |
| Bush bean | 15-20 | Sow in two batches |
| Onion | 30-40 | Keeps until spring |

Hortulus uses the same figures in **Autofill**: enter your household size, time and water and the planner proposes a layout.

## 2. Know your area and resources

- **Sun**: most fruiting crops need at least 6 hours of direct sun. Leafy crops tolerate partial shade.
- **Water**: in a dry summer week a vegetable garden needs 15-30 litres per m². A 200 L water butt empties quickly.
- **Time**: count 5-10 minutes per m² per week in the growing season (weeding, checking, harvesting), plus extra for tomatoes (side shoots, tying in).
- **Compost**: 3-5 kg per m² per year for hungry crops.

## 3. Divide into beds and rotate

Use permanent beds 1.0-1.2 m wide so you can reach the middle from both sides without stepping on the soil. Change the plant family in each bed every year (see *Crop rotation*).

## 4. Tall on the north side

Put tall crops (climbing beans, sweetcorn, tomatoes) on the **north side** of a bed (southern hemisphere: south side) so they do not shade lower crops. Hortulus warns in the planner when a tall crop shades its neighbour.

## 5. Plan in time

A bed is rarely full all year. Quick crops (radish, lettuce, spinach) fit before or after a main crop. Use the **season overview** (timeline) to fill empty spells, and sow green manure at the end of summer.

## Winter checklist

- Review last year's notes and harvests.
- Check seed stock, do a germination test.
- Check the rotation and draw the new layout.
- Order seed and sets.
- Service tools and set up the sowing station.
