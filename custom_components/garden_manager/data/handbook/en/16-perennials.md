# Perennial vegetables and soft fruit

Perennial vegetables (asparagus, rhubarb, artichoke, sorrel, perennial leek) and soft fruit (strawberry, raspberry, currants) stay in the same place for years. They are outside the rotation but need yearly care.

## A permanent place

Give perennials their own bed or the edge of the garden so they don't block the rotation of the annual beds. Hortulus takes this into account in **Autofill**.

## Yearly care

| Task | When | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Top-dress with compost | Autumn or early spring | They deplete the same soil for years |
| Cut back | After the first frosts or in spring | Less disease, fresh growth |
| Divide / rejuvenate | Every 3-4 years | Old clumps turn woody and less productive |
| Winter protection | Before the first hard frost | Artichoke, rosemary, potted bay |

## Examples

- **Rhubarb**: no harvest in the first year; after that until late June. Break off flower stalks.
- **Asparagus**: three years' patience; then cut for 6-8 weeks.
- **Strawberry**: remove runners (or use them as new plants), a new bed every 3 years.
- **Raspberry**: summer varieties - cut fruited canes after harvest; autumn varieties - cut everything to the ground in winter.
