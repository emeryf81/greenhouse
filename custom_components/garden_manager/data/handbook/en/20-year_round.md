# Vegetables all year round

With a little planning you harvest something fresh every month - even in January. The keys: **succession sowing**, **winter crops**, **storage crops** and **the greenhouse as an extension**. The Advice screen shows per month whether there is something to harvest and suggests crops to fill the gaps.

## Four strategies

1. **Succession sowing** - a little lettuce, radish, spinach, beans, carrots and rocket every 2-4 weeks. In the Planner: *Succession*.
2. **Follow-on crops** - a bed that comes free in July (early potato, peas, broad beans) still has room for leeks, kale, winter carrots, endive, Chinese cabbage, turnip greens or a green manure. The task *Sow for autumn and winter harvests* reminds you in July.
3. **Winter crops** - leeks, kale, sprouts, cavolo nero, winter purslane, lamb's lettuce, parsnip and salsify stay outdoors all winter and get sweeter after frost.
4. **Storage** - potatoes, onions, garlic, squash (Hokkaido!), celeriac, beetroot, carrots in sand, apples and pears. Cool, dark, frost-free; check monthly.

## The greenhouse in winter

An unheated greenhouse is 3-5 °C warmer in winter and, above all, dry and sheltered. Sow late August - September: lamb's lettuce, winter purslane, spinach, pak choi, mizuna, rocket, chard, spring onion and lettuce for March. Ventilate on sunny days, water sparingly and lay fleece over the crops in hard frost.

## The hungry gap (March - May)

The hardest weeks: the winter store is empty and the spring harvest has not started. Bridge it with purple sprouting broccoli, rhubarb, asparagus, perennial herbs (chives, wild garlic, sorrel), turnip greens and spinach under glass, and sprouted seeds on the windowsill.
