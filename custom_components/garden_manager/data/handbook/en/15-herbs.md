# Herbs

Herbs are the easiest and most rewarding plants in the garden: few pests, little feeding and a big effect in the kitchen. Their flowers also attract beneficial insects.

## Three groups

| Group | Examples | Position | Soil |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mediterranean, woody | rosemary, thyme, sage, lavender, oregano | Full sun, sheltered | Poor, free-draining, rather dry |
| Moisture-loving, leafy | parsley, chives, mint, chervil, lovage | Sun to part shade | Rich, moist |
| Annual, fast | basil, dill, coriander, chervil | Sun (basil: warm!) | Normal, evenly moist |

## Tips

- **Never feed Mediterranean herbs** and keep them on the dry side: that's when they are most aromatic and survive winter best. Wet feet in winter kill more often than frost.
- **Mint** spreads: always in a pot or a sunken bucket without a bottom.
- **Basil** is frost tender and wants warmth; it grows best in the greenhouse or in a pot in a sunny spot.
- **Dill and coriander** bolt quickly in hot weather: sow a little every 3-4 weeks (succession).
- **Parsley** germinates slowly (3-4 weeks): soak the seed overnight.
- Cut **often**: plants stay compact and make new shoots.

## Pruning and dividing

- Woody herbs (thyme, sage, lavender, rosemary): cut back in spring to just above old wood - never into old wood.
- Clump-forming perennial herbs (chives, mint, lovage, lemon balm, tarragon): **divide** every 3 years - lift, split, replant the youngest parts.
- Cut spent stems in autumn.

Hortulus schedules these tasks based on each herb's traits.

## Drying and storing

Harvest herbs in the morning once the dew has dried, just before flowering (most aroma). Dry in bunches in a dark, airy place or freeze in ice cubes with oil or water.
