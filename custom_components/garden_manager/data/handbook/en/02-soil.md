# Soil and compost

Healthy plants grow in living soil. Soil is not a container you pour food into, but an ecosystem of worms, fungi and bacteria that make nutrients available.

## Soil types

| Soil | Character | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Sand | Warms fast, dries fast, leaches | Lots of compost, mulch, smaller and more frequent watering |
| Loam | Ideal: holds water and stays airy | Keep it topped up with compost |
| Clay | Rich but heavy and cold; sticky wet, cracks dry | Never work it wet, compost and green manures |
| Peat | High organic matter, acidic | Lime as needed, good drainage |

**Available water** varies a lot: sand holds about 80 mm of water per metre of root zone, loam 160 mm, clay 180 mm. Hortulus uses these values in the **water balance** when no moisture sensor is fitted.

## pH

Most vegetables grow best at pH 6.0-7.0. Brassicas want 6.5-7.5 (which suppresses clubroot), potatoes tolerate 5.5. Test the soil every three years; Hortulus puts that task on the calendar.

## Preparing a bed

1. **Don't dig** unless you must: digging breaks soil structure and brings weed seed up. Loosening with a fork or broadfork is enough.
2. **Remove weeds**, especially perennial roots (couch grass, bindweed).
3. **Spread compost** and rake it in lightly two weeks before sowing or planting.
4. **Rake to a fine tilth** for small seed (carrot, lettuce).

Hortulus creates a *Prepare bed for planting* task two weeks before the next crop, with the right amount of compost.

## Making compost

- Mix **greens** (kitchen scraps, grass: nitrogen rich) and **browns** (leaves, straw, cardboard: carbon rich) roughly 1:2.
- Keep the heap as moist as a wrung-out sponge.
- Turn it 2-3 times a year for air.
- After 6-12 months the compost is ready: dark, crumbly, smells of forest floor.
- No diseased plants, seeding weeds or meat.

## Green manures

Never leave soil bare. A green manure protects against leaching and erosion, feeds soil life and suppresses weeds.

| Green manure | Sow | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Phacelia | March-September | Bee plant, unrelated to vegetables |
| Mustard | August-September | Fast; **not** before brassicas (clubroot) |
| Clover | April-August | Fixes nitrogen |
| Winter rye | September-October | Hardy, deep roots |
| Vetch | August-September | Fixes nitrogen, hardy |

Cut green manure before it flowers, let it wilt for a few days and work it in shallowly or leave it as mulch.
