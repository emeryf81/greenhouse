# Fruit trees and soft fruit

Fruit is the most lasting investment in a garden: an apple tree planted today still crops in thirty years. Hortulus therefore treats fruit as **perennial**: a tree that is in your plan in year 1 is still there in year 2, 3, … until you remove it explicitly. Every year you automatically get the right care tasks: winter pruning, feeding, thinning, netting, harvesting and clearing up.

## Planting

- **When:** bare-root trees and bushes from late October to mid-March, when the soil is not frozen or waterlogged. Container plants can go in all year but need lots of water in summer.
- **Planting hole:** twice as wide as the root ball, just as deep. Loosen the bottom and sides, mix some compost into the soil (no fresh manure).
- **Stake:** drive the stake in on the windward side before planting, tie the tree with a flexible tie in a figure of eight.
- **Graft union** (the kink low on the trunk) stays a hand's width above the soil.
- **Tree circle:** keep a 1 m circle around the trunk free of grass and mulch it. Grass is the biggest competitor of young trees.

## Rootstock and form

The rootstock decides how big a tree gets. For small gardens: apple on M9 or M26 (2-3 m), pear on quince, cherry on Gisela 5, plum on Pixy or St. Julien A. Trained forms (cordon, fan, espalier) fit against a wall or fence and crop early.

## Pollination

Much fruit is **self-sterile**: you need a second variety flowering at the same time within about 50 m (the neighbours' tree counts).

| Fruit | Pollination | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Apple | Partner needed | A crab apple is an excellent pollinator |
| Pear | Partner needed | Conference is partly self-fertile |
| Sweet cherry | Usually partner | Stella, Lapins, Sunburst are self-fertile |
| Sour cherry, plum, peach | Mostly self-fertile | Still more fruit with a second tree |
| Blueberry, honeyberry, kiwi berry | Partner needed | Kiwi berry: 1 male per 6 females |
| Red/white currant, gooseberry, raspberry | Self-fertile | Insects raise the yield |

Hortulus warns in the advice when a tree has no pollinator nearby. Help the pollinators: leave dandelions flowering, hang a mason-bee house and **never spray during bloom**.

## Pruning calendar

| What | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| Apple, pear (winter) | December - February, dry frost-free day | remove dead, crossing and inward wood; shorten leaders by 1/3 |
| Apple, pear (summer) | late July - August | new side shoots to 3 leaves: more fruit buds, less growth |
| Cherry, plum, peach, apricot | after harvest, in leaf (July - August) | never in winter: silver leaf and canker |
| Red/white currant, gooseberry | winter | keep 8-10 stems, side shoots to 2 buds |
| Blackcurrant | winter | cut 1/3 of the oldest stems at the base |
| Summer raspberry | after harvest | remove fruited canes, tie in 6-8 new ones per metre |
| Autumn raspberry | February | everything to the ground |
| Grape | mid-winter + summer | spur prune to 1-2 buds; in summer stop 2 leaves past the bunch |

Disinfect secateurs between trees with alcohol or a flame. Don't paint large wounds: a clean cut at the branch collar heals best.

## Thinning

A tree often sets more fruit than it can feed. After the **June drop** thin apples and pears to 1-2 fruits per cluster, 10-15 cm apart. You get bigger fruit and avoid **biennial bearing** (one year a glut, next year nothing).

## Feeding and mulching

Fruit mainly needs **potassium** for flavour and storage. Late February: 3-5 l compost per m² under the canopy plus a handful of wood ash, or comfrey leaves as mulch. Blueberries and cranberries want acid soil: pine needles, bark, coffee grounds and rain water; never lime.

## Ecological crop protection

- **Codling moth:** corrugated cardboard band round the trunk from June (pupating caterpillars hide in it), nest boxes for tits.
- **Winter moth:** grease band at 1 m height from October to March.
- **Aphids:** soft soap (20 g/l), ladybirds, earwig pots (flower pot stuffed with straw, upside down in the tree).
- **Scab:** resistant varieties (Topaz, Santana, Rubinola), clear fallen leaves, horsetail tea as a tonic.
- **Brown rot:** remove mummified fruit and wilted shoots straight away.
- **Peach leaf curl:** rain shelter from leaf fall until April, resistant varieties.
- **Birds:** net (fine mesh, pegged down) a few weeks before ripening.
- **Spotted wing drosophila:** pick daily, remove overripe fruit, fine insect mesh.

## Harvest and storage

Apples are ready when they come off with a quarter turn and the pips are brown. Pick pears slightly unripe and ripen them indoors. Store fruit cool (2-5 °C), dark and humid, apart from vegetables (ethylene). Check the store every month.
