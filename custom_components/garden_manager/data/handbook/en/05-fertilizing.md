# Feeding

Plants mainly need **nitrogen (N)** for leaves, **phosphorus (P)** for roots and flowers, and **potassium (K)** for fruit, flavour and resilience - plus trace elements such as magnesium and calcium.

## Hungry, moderate, light feeders

| Group | Examples | Compost per m² | Organic base fertiliser |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hungry | tomato, courgette, squash, cabbage, leek, celery | 5 kg | 80 g |
| Moderate | carrot, beetroot, lettuce, spinach, onion | 3 kg | 40 g |
| Light | herbs, radish, lamb's lettuce | 1.5 kg | - |
| Nitrogen fixers | pea, bean, broad bean | 0.5 kg | - |

Hortulus calculates the compost per bed from its area and the hungriest crop that follows.

## Feeding during growth

Fruiting crops (tomato, pepper, cucumber) get a potassium-rich liquid feed every 10-14 days from the first flowers. Hortulus puts the amount in the task: litres of diluted feed, or grams of granular fertiliser.

**Make your own**: nettle tea (1 kg nettles in 10 L water, ferment for 2 weeks, dilute 1:10) is rich in nitrogen; comfrey tea in potassium.

## Measuring: EC and NPK

- An **EC sensor** measures the salt content of soil moisture (mS/cm). Too low: lack of nutrients. Too high (above 2.5-3): salt stress, roots struggle to take up water.
- An **NPK sensor** gives an indication of available nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium.

Hortulus combines these readings with the needs of the crops in the bed into a **nutrient balance index** (0-100) and warns about deficiency or excess.

## Recognising deficiencies

| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Older leaves yellow, pale plant | Nitrogen |
| Purplish leaf undersides | Phosphorus (often cold soil) |
| Brown leaf edges, poor fruit | Potassium |
| Yellow between the veins of old leaves | Magnesium |
| Black base on tomato fruit | Calcium, caused by irregular watering |

**Careful**: too much nitrogen gives lush leaves, little fruit and more aphids.
