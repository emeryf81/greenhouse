# Food forest

A **food forest** (forest garden) imitates a young woodland edge: several layers of plants that feed, shelter and protect each other. Once established it needs little work and gives fruit, nuts, greens and herbs year after year.

## The seven layers

1. **Canopy:** tall fruit and nut trees (walnut, chestnut, standard apple).
2. **Sub-canopy:** half-standard fruit, elder, quince.
3. **Shrubs:** currants, raspberries, hazel.
4. **Herbs and perennials:** rhubarb, mint, lemon balm, comfrey.
5. **Ground cover:** strawberries, wild garlic, creeping bugle.
6. **Roots and tubers:** Jerusalem artichoke, horseradish, shallots.
7. **Climbers:** hardy kiwi, grape, hops.

## In Hortulus

Give a zone the type **Food forest**. Put beds or planting areas in it and plant perennial crops. The advice shows which layers are still missing and suggests plants. An orchard can grow into a food forest step by step.

## Tips

- Start with the trees and shrubs: they set light and shade.
- Plant nitrogen fixers (alder, Elaeagnus, clover) between the fruit trees.
- Keep the soil covered with mulch or ground cover at all times.
- Plant in autumn: bare-root trees and shrubs establish best.
