# Ecological gardening and permaculture

Ecological gardening works **with** nature instead of against it. Permaculture goes one step further: you design the garden as a small ecosystem that largely looks after itself. None of this is required in Hortulus, but every bit helps. Switch the tips on or off under **Settings → Ecological tips**.

## The basics

- **Keep the soil covered.** Mulch, green manure or plants: bare soil washes out, dries out and loses its life.
- **No dig.** Feed the soil from above with compost (see *No-dig and mulching*).
- **Diversity.** Mix families, herbs and flowers in every bed. A monoculture attracts that family's pests.
- **Welcome the helpers.** Hedgehogs, toads, birds, ground beetles, hoverflies, parasitic wasps and ladybirds are your best pest control.
- **Close the loops.** Compost, harvest rainwater, save your own seed, leaves on the beds.

## Organic intervention

1. **Look and wait first.** A few aphids feed ladybird larvae.
2. **By hand.** Pick off, hose off, net or fleece.
3. **Plant extracts.** Cold nettle extract against aphids, horsetail tea against fungi, garlic infusion.
4. **Release beneficials** in the greenhouse: Encarsia against whitefly, predatory mites against spider mite.

## Nettle tea and herbal extracts

- **Nettle tea (feed):** 1 kg fresh nettles in 10 l rainwater, ferment 1-2 weeks, stir daily. Dilute 1:10.
- **Cold nettle extract (against aphids):** 1 kg nettles, 10 l water, 24 hours, spray undiluted.
- **Horsetail tea (against fungi):** boil 150 g dried horsetail for 30 minutes in 10 l water, dilute 1:5.
- **Comfrey tea:** like nettle tea, rich in potassium for tomatoes and beans.

## Make room for wildlife

- A wild corner, brush pile or leaf heap for hedgehogs and insects.
- Leave hollow stems standing until spring.
- A pond or drinking dish, nest boxes and an insect hotel.
- Native hedges instead of fences.

## Permaculture zones

Put what you need often close to the house (herbs, lettuce) and what needs little care further away (fruit trees, food forest, wild corner). In the Garden Planner you draw the site first and the zones next, so this logic follows naturally.
