# Companion planting

Some plants help each other, others get in each other's way. Companion planting is part science and part gardeners' experience, so Hortulus always shows **why** a combination scores well or badly.

## How Hortulus scores

The planner looks at plants within the **influence radius** (60 cm by default) and weighs:

1. **Known pairs** from the plant database (tomato + basil: good; potato + tomato: bad, they share blight).
2. **Family relations** when there is no specific rule (alliums next to legumes hold the beans back).
3. **Pest deterrence**: a plant that repels a neighbour's pest (chives against carrot fly, French marigold against eelworm) or lures it away as a trap crop (nasturtium for aphids).
4. **Allelopathy**: plants whose root exudates suppress neighbours (fennel, wormwood, sunflower).
5. **Competition**: two hungry crops close together share the same nutrients.
6. **Shade**: a tall crop on the sunny side of a low crop takes its light - unless the low crop tolerates shade (lettuce actually likes it in summer).

Distance matters: the closer, the stronger the effect. When there are conflicts Hortulus suggests **better neighbours**.

## Classic good pairs

| Pair | Why |
|---|---|
| Carrot + onion/leek | Scents confuse carrot fly and onion fly |
| Tomato + basil | Basil repels whitefly; similar needs |
| Sweetcorn + climbing bean + squash ("three sisters") | Support, nitrogen and ground cover |
| Cabbage + celery or dill | Confusing scents against cabbage white |
| Beans + summer savory | Repels black bean aphid |
| French marigold anywhere | Eelworm, attracts hoverflies |

## Avoid

| Pair | Why |
|---|---|
| Potato + tomato | Same disease (blight) |
| Onion/garlic + beans/peas | Stunt legumes |
| Fennel + almost anything | Allelopathic |
| Cabbage + strawberry | Compete, pests |

Companion planting doesn't replace rotation or good soil care, but it raises biodiversity: more flowers and scents mean more beneficial insects.
