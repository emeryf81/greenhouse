# Tools and materials

Good tools need not be expensive, but they must be solid and sharp. Buy one good spade rather than three bad ones. In the task list Hortulus shows the tools and materials each job needs; the **shopping list** (Advice › Supplies) works out what to buy this season.

## The basic kit

| Tool | For | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Digging fork / broadfork | loosening soil without turning it | spares your back and the soil life |
| Spade | planting holes, edging | keep it sharp with a file |
| Hoe (draw or Dutch hoe) | weeding, breaking a crust | weekly in dry weather |
| Rake | fine seed bed, spreading mulch | |
| Trowel and dibber | planting out, leeks and onions | |
| Secateurs (bypass) | pruning, harvesting | disinfect between plants |
| Watering can with a fine rose + hose with spray head | sowing and watering | use rain water |
| Wheelbarrow or garden cart | compost, mulch, harvest | |
| Plant labels and a pencil | knowing what is where | pencil stays readable |
| Garden twine / jute | tying in | |
| Seed trays, pots, seed compost | sowing indoors | |
| Gloves, kneeler | | |

## Handy for keen gardeners

- **Soil thermometer** - sow only when the soil is warm enough.
- **Min/max thermometer** in the greenhouse.
- **Soil moisture meter** or wireless sensor (connects to Home Assistant).
- **Sowing board / line** for straight rows, **row marker**.
- **Grow light and heat mat** for sowing indoors.
- **Loppers and pruning saw** for fruit trees; **three-legged ladder** for picking.
- **Soaker hose and timer** (or irrigation through Home Assistant).
- **Compost sieve** and a **compost thermometer**.

## Materials per season

| Material | When | How much (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|
| Compost | spring and autumn | 3-5 l/m² per year (about 2-3 kg) |
| Organic fertiliser (pellets) | for hungry crops | 50-100 g/m² |
| Lime (dolomite) | every 3 years if pH < 6 | 100-200 g/m², never with manure |
| Fleece (17-30 g/m²) | frost, early crops, insects | bed width + 50 cm |
| Insect mesh (0.8 mm) | brassicas, carrots, onions | bed width + 60 cm |
| Shade netting 30-40 % or shade paint | greenhouse, salads in heat | roof area |
| Straw or grass clippings | mulch | 5-8 cm layer |
| Bamboo canes, tomato spirals | beans, tomatoes | 1 per plant |
| Bird netting | fruit, brassica seedlings | fine mesh |
| Green manure seed | autumn | 2-3 g/m² (phacelia), 15-20 g/m² (rye) |

## Make your own feeds and remedies

| What | Recipe | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Nettle feed (nitrogen) | 1 kg young nettles in 10 l rain water, ferment 10-14 days | 1:10 for leafy crops, 1:20 for seedlings |
| Comfrey feed (potassium) | 1 kg leaves in 10 l water, 3 weeks | 1:10 for tomato, courgette, fruit |
| Compost tea | a spadeful of mature compost in a hessian bag, 3 days in a barrel of rain water | water undiluted |
| Wood ash (potassium, lime) | ash of untreated wood | 50-100 g/m², not for potatoes (scab) |
| Coffee grounds | spread out, dried | slightly acid, for blueberries; worms love it |
| Eggshells | dried and ground | lime, around brassicas; coarse against slugs |
| Urine (diluted) | 1:10 with water, fresh | rich in nitrogen, on the soil, not on leaves |
| Grass clippings | thin layers | mulch and slow nitrogen |
| Leaf mould | leaves rotted for 1-2 years | seed compost, soil improver |

## Tool care

After each use: brush off the soil and dry. Once a year (November): sharpen, remove rust with steel wool, oil the metal lightly and rub wooden handles with linseed oil. Disinfect secateurs between diseased plants with alcohol.
