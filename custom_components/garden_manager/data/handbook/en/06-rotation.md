# Crop rotation

When the same plant family grows in the same place year after year, diseases and pests build up in the soil (clubroot in brassicas, eelworm in potatoes, white rot in onions) and the soil is drained one-sidedly.

## The rule

Return a family to the same bed only after **3 to 4 years**. Hortulus tracks which family grew in each bed and warns:

- **Error**: the family returns far too soon.
- **Warning**: one year too soon.

It also suggests **alternative beds** where the crop does fit.

## A classic four-year plan

| Year | Bed A | Bed B | Bed C | Bed D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nightshades (tomato, potato) | Legumes | Brassicas | Roots and alliums |
| 2 | Legumes | Brassicas | Roots/alliums | Nightshades |
| 3 | Brassicas | Roots/alliums | Nightshades | Legumes |
| 4 | Roots/alliums | Nightshades | Legumes | Brassicas |

The order makes sense: legumes leave nitrogen for the hungry brassicas, followed by the less demanding root crops.

## Families worth knowing

- **Nightshades**: tomato, potato, pepper, aubergine.
- **Brassicas**: all cabbages, radish, rocket, turnip, mustard (also as green manure!).
- **Legumes**: peas, beans, clover.
- **Umbellifers**: carrot, parsnip, celery, fennel, parsley, dill.
- **Alliums**: onion, leek, garlic, shallot, chives.
- **Cucurbits**: courgette, cucumber, squash, melon.
- **Amaranth family**: beetroot, chard, spinach.
- **Daisy family**: lettuce, endive, chicory.

Perennials (asparagus, rhubarb, most herbs) are outside the rotation.
