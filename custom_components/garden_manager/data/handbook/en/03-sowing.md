# Sowing and planting out

## Frost dates as anchors

Every sowing window in Hortulus is expressed **relative to the last spring frost** and **the first autumn frost** of your region. In the Low Countries the average last frost is around 20 April; tender crops traditionally wait until after the **Ice Saints** (11-15 May).

So "8-6 weeks before last frost" for tomato means sowing indoors in late February or early March. Change your region or hemisphere and every date moves with it.

## Sowing indoors

- Use clean trays and **seed compost** (fine and low in nutrients), not potting compost.
- Sow at the depth given for the crop: as a rule 2-3 times the seed's thickness. Light germinators (lettuce, celery) barely covered.
- **Soil temperature** drives germination: tomato and pepper want 20-25 °C, lettuce germinates poorly above 25 °C.
- Once seedlings emerge: lots of light and slightly cooler (16-18 °C) to prevent legginess.
- **Prick out** into their own pots when the first true leaf appears.

## Hardening off

Plants raised indoors must get used to sun, wind and temperature swings. Put them out for **7-10 days**, first a few hours in the shade, then longer and sunnier. Plants for the greenhouse need no hardening off.

## Sowing direct

- Root crops (carrot, parsnip, radish) and legumes are usually sown in place: they dislike transplanting.
- Wait for **warm soil**: Hortulus compares the soil temperature sensor with the crop's minimum and postpones sowing when it is too cold.
- Keep the seedbed moist until germination. A board or fleece over slow germinators (carrot) helps.
- **Thin** to the right spacing once seedlings can be handled.

## Planting out

- Plant on an overcast day or in the evening.
- Water the rootball well beforehand.
- Tomatoes can go deeper (to the first leaf): they root along the stem. Firm brassicas in well.
- Respect the spacing: too close means more disease and less harvest.

## Succession

Quick crops (lettuce, radish, spinach, rocket) are better sown **a little every 2-3 weeks** than a lot at once. Hortulus can split a planting into successive sowings.
