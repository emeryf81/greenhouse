# Harvest and storage

## The right moment

Hortulus estimates the harvest date from the sowing or planting date and the crop's days to maturity. With a temperature sensor it also counts **degree days**: every day warmer than the crop's base temperature (for example 10 °C for tomato) adds up. A warm spring brings the harvest forward by days, a cold one delays it. Heat stress (above the crop's maximum) delays fruiting crops.

| Crop | Ready when |
|---|---|
| Tomato | Fully coloured, slightly soft |
| Courgette | 15-20 cm; bigger = less flavour |
| Bean | Pod snaps cleanly, seeds still small |
| Carrot | Shoulder 2-3 cm wide |
| Onion | Foliage falls over and dries |
| Potato (storage) | Foliage dead, skin set |
| Squash | Stalk woody, sounds hollow |

## Pick often

Beans, courgettes, peas, cucumbers and herbs keep producing as long as you pick. Wait too long and the plant sets seed and stops.

## Storage

| Crop | How | How long |
|---|---|---|
| Potato | Dark, 4-8 °C, frost free | 4-6 months |
| Onion, garlic | Cure for 2 weeks, then airy and dry | 6-8 months |
| Carrot, beetroot, celeriac | In damp sand in a cellar, or leave in the ground | 3-5 months |
| Squash | Cure 10 days in the sun, then 10-15 °C | 4-8 months |
| Cabbage | Cool and humid, or leave standing (kale, sprouts) | weeks - months |
| Beans, peas | Blanch and freeze | 12 months |
| Herbs | Dry in bunches or freeze in oil | 6-12 months |

## Record your harvest

When ticking off a harvest task, note how much you picked. Next year you'll see what was worth growing.
